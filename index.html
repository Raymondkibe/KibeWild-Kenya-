Maasai Mara National Reserve250 km · 5–6 hr

Home of the Great Wildebeest Migration (1.5 million animals). 95+ mammal species, 570+ bird species. Big Cat capital of Africa — the highest lion density outside fully protected areas.

Resorts & Camps: Angama Mara, &Beyond Kichwa Tembo, Sarova Mara, Ashnil Mara Camp, Fairmont Mara Safari Club.

Operators: Gamewatchers Safaris, Heritage Safaris, Basecamp Explorer.

Highlight: Mara River crocodile crossings July–October.

Amboseli National Park220 km · 4 hr

Iconic elephant herds against the backdrop of Mount Kilimanjaro (5,895 m). 420+ bird species, 50+ mammal species. Famous for large-tusked "big tusker" bull elephants.

Resorts: Ol Tukai Lodge, Amboseli Serena Safari Lodge, Tortilis Camp, Tawi Lodge.

Highlight: Observation Hill at sunset — Kilimanjaro panorama.

KWS Official Data
Tsavo East & Tsavo West330 km · 5 hr

Kenya's largest national park. Famous red elephants (coated in volcanic red soil), dramatic lava flows, Mudanda Rock. 600+ bird species. Larger population of elephants than any other park.

Camps: Satao Camp, Kilaguni Serena, Voi Safari Lodge, Finch Hattons.

Highlight: Galana River, Lugard Falls, and Mzima Springs.

KWS Official Data
Lake Nakuru National Park160 km · 2.5 hr

Famous flamingo lake and black & white rhino sanctuary. 450+ bird species — one of the best birding sites in Africa. The alkaline lake turns pink with flamingos in peak season.

Resorts: Lake Nakuru Lodge, Flamingo Hill Camp, Sarova Lion Hill Lodge.

Highlight: Baboon Cliff escarpment views over the whole lake.

KWS Official Data
Samburu National Reserve340 km · 6 hr

The "Special Five" — Grevy's zebra, reticulated giraffe, Beisa oryx, gerenuk, and Somali ostrich. Found only north of the equator. Ewaso Nyiro River brings diverse game to the banks year-round.

Resorts: Samburu Game Lodge, Larsen's Tented Camp, Elephant Bedroom Camp.

KWS Official Data
Meru National Park350 km · 6 hr

Wild and less crowded. Famous as the home of Elsa the lioness (Born Free). White rhinos, black rhinos, wild dogs, diverse primates. 13 rivers and permanent swamps.

Camps: Elsa's Kopje (luxury), Leopard Rock Lodge.

KWS Official Data
Lake Naivasha & Hell's Gate100 km · 1.5 hr

Cycle among hippos and giraffes at Hell's Gate (inspired The Lion King's Pride Rock). Geothermal landscape, dramatic Njorowa Gorge. Lake Naivasha has 1,000+ hippos and 340+ bird species.

Resorts: Lake Naivasha Sopa Resort, Crayfish Camp, Kiangazi House.

KWS Official Data

Home to the last two northern white rhinos on Earth (Sudan's legacy). Largest black rhino sanctuary in East Africa. Also has chimpanzees — unique for Kenya.

Camps: Ol Pejeta Bush Camp, Sweetwaters Serena Camp.

Kenya's Mystical Forests

Kakamega Forest

Kenya's Only Tropical Rainforest

Isolated remnant of the ancient Guineo-Congolian rainforest, covering 240 km². Home to 400+ bird species, 7 primate species including the De Brazza's monkey and colobus, plus 350+ butterfly species.

  • Giant forest hog — Africa's largest wild pig
  • Red-tailed monkey, Blue monkey, Olive baboon
  • Great Blue Turaco, African Goshawk (400+ birds)
  • Guided night walks: owls, bushbabies, chameleons

Aberdare & Mount Kenya Forests

UNESCO World Heritage Site

Misty bamboo highlands between 2,000–4,000 m. Home to the melanistic (black) leopard — one of Kenya's most elusive animals. The Bongo antelope lives only here and at Mount Kenya.

  • Black (melanistic) leopard — extremely rare
  • Giant forest elephant, Bongo antelope, Bushbuck
  • Eastern black rhino sanctuary
  • Sykes monkey, Giant forest hog, Serval cat

Coastal Gems: Mombasa & Malindi

Beyond the savannah, Kenya's 480 km of Indian Ocean coastline is one of Africa's best-kept secrets — white sand, turquoise water, and ancient Swahili culture.

Mombasa

Kenya's Oldest Coastal City · Founded c.900 AD

Kenya's ancient port city blends centuries of Swahili, Arab, Portuguese, and British heritage. Fort Jesus (UNESCO World Heritage) guards the old harbour. Diani Beach has been voted Africa's Leading Beach Destination multiple times by World Travel Awards.

Malindi & Watamu

Marine National Park · UNESCO Biosphere Reserve

A laid-back paradise 120 km north of Mombasa. The Malindi Marine National Park — Kenya's oldest marine park (est. 1968) — protects pristine coral reefs, sea turtles, dolphins, whale sharks, and manta rays. Watamu is nearby and equally stunning.

Lamu Island

UNESCO World Heritage Site · Oldest Living Swahili Town

Lamu Old Town is one of the best-preserved Swahili settlements in East Africa, inhabited since the 12th century. No cars — only donkeys and dhows. Experience the Lamu Cultural Festival (November), one of Kenya's most celebrated events.

  • Dhow sailing across the Lamu Archipelago
  • Swahili cooking classes and cultural tours
  • Shela Beach — pristine, uncrowded
  • Lamu Museum and Fort (built 1813)

Kenya's Most Iconic Birds

Lesser Flamingo

Lake Nakuru · Lake Bogoria

Over 1 million gather at Lake Bogoria making it the world's largest flamingo concentration — a pink carpet across the alkaline waters.

African Fish Eagle

Lake Naivasha · Lake Victoria

Kenya's national bird symbol. Its haunting cry over lakes is the iconic sound of East Africa. Powerful, white-headed raptor with a 2 m wingspan.

Superb Starling

Nationwide · Very Common

Arguably the most beautiful bird in Kenya — iridescent blue-green back, chestnut belly, white band. One of the first birds every safari visitor spots.

Secretary Bird

Maasai Mara · Amboseli

A raptor that walks — hunts snakes on foot across open grasslands. Unmistakable with its long legs and ornamental head feathers. Listed as Vulnerable (IUCN).

Lilac-breasted Roller

Mara · Samburu · Tsavo

Often called Africa's most colourful bird — 8 colours in one bird. Dives acrobatically mid-air during courtship display. A safari photography favourite.

Great Blue Turaco

Kakamega Forest

Africa's largest turaco at 75 cm. Stunning turquoise-blue plumage. Restricted to Kakamega — a must-see for birdwatchers visiting western Kenya.

Kori Bustard

Maasai Mara · Samburu

The world's heaviest flying bird — can weigh up to 20 kg. Walks slowly through grasslands. The male's courtship display, puffing neck feathers, is spectacular.

Ostrich (Somali)

Samburu · Tsavo · Meru

The Somali ostrich (northern Kenya) has blue-grey neck and legs, distinct from the Masai ostrich. World's largest bird at up to 2.7 m, can run at 70 km/h.

Kenya's Environmental Heroes

From Nobel laureates to grassroots defenders — these champions protect Kenya's forests, wildlife, and land.

Wangari Maathai

Nobel Peace Prize 2004 · Green Belt Movement

First African woman to win the Nobel Peace Prize. Founded the Green Belt Movement, planting over 51 million trees while championing women's rights and democracy across Kenya. Born in Nyeri, 1940.

Truphena Muthoni

Youth Climate Activist · Tree Planter

Planted over 11,000 trees and staged a 72-hour tree-hugging protest that went viral globally, inspiring a new generation of environmental defenders. A leading voice for Kenya's forests.

Phyllis Omido

Goldman Environmental Prize 2015

Led the grassroots fight against lead poisoning in Mombasa's Owino Uhuru slum, forcing closure of a toxic smelter and saving thousands of children from permanent harm to their health.

James Wakibia

Anti-Plastic Campaigner · #SayNoToPlastics

Key activist behind Kenya's 2017 plastic bag ban — one of the world's strictest environmental laws. His #SayNoToPlastics campaign spread across Africa, protecting marine and forest ecosystems.

Paula Kahumbu

CEO, WildlifeDirect

Led the "Hands Off Our Elephants" campaign with First Lady Margaret Kenyatta. A powerful global voice against ivory poaching and for Kenya's wildlife protection laws and conservation education.

Elizabeth Wathuti

Founder, Green Generation Initiative

Youth climate activist who planted thousands of trees across Kenya and delivered a powerful speech at COP26 in Glasgow, moving world leaders to tears with her appeal for immediate climate action.

Richard Leakey

Former KWS Director · Paleoanthropologist

Burned 12 tonnes of ivory in 1989 in a symbolic act that reshaped global opinion on the ivory trade. Revolutionised KWS anti-poaching, saving Kenya's elephants and rhinos from the brink of extinction.

Wanjira Mathai

WRI Africa · Wangari's Daughter

Continues her mother Wangari's green legacy, leading global forest restoration and climate resilience programmes as VP & Regional Director of the World Resources Institute Africa.

Kivutha Kibwana

Former Governor, Makueni County

Pioneered community water harvesting pans in arid Makueni County, transforming food security and water access for thousands. A model for climate-smart governance across East Africa.

Great Migration 2026

Best time to witness river crossings.

Mara River crossings peak July–October. Over 1.5 million wildebeest, 200,000 zebras, and 350,000 gazelles make the annual circuit. The most dramatic crossings happen where crocodiles wait — Serena Crossing and Lookout Hill are Raymond's top picks. Book July–August for peak action.

Frequently Asked Questions

Best time for safari?

June–October (dry season) is optimal for wildlife viewing as animals gather around water sources. July–October for the Great Migration river crossings. January–March for calving season and excellent big cat sightings. April–May (long rains) is quieter and cheaper.

Who is Wangari Maathai?

Professor Wangari Maathai (1940–2011) was a Kenyan environmental activist and Nobel Peace Prize laureate (2004). She founded the Green Belt Movement in 1977, which planted over 51 million trees across Kenya. She was the first African woman and first environmentalist to win the Nobel Peace Prize.

Best beaches in Kenya?

Diani Beach (south of Mombasa) is consistently voted Africa's best beach. Watamu is a UNESCO Biosphere Reserve with excellent snorkelling. Lamu Island offers unspoilt Swahili culture. Tiwi Beach is quieter and budget-friendly. All are on the Indian Ocean with warm water year-round.

Why is Kenya great for birdwatching?

Kenya has 1,136 recorded bird species — over 10% of all bird species on Earth. Diverse habitats from coastal mangroves to alpine moorland create extraordinary variety. Lake Nakuru, Kakamega Forest, and the Maasai Mara are world-renowned sites. Over 1 million flamingos can be seen at Lake Bogoria.

How much does a Kenya safari cost?

Budget safaris start from $100–200/day (camping/tented camps). Mid-range lodges run $200–500/day. Luxury experiences (Angama Mara, &Beyond) cost $500–1,500+/day. Park fees are set by KWS: Maasai Mara $100/day adults, Amboseli $90/day. Contact Raymond for tailored packages.

Do I need a visa for Kenya?

Kenya operates an eVisa system. Apply online at evisa.go.ke before travel. Most nationalities receive approval within 3–5 business days. Single entry: $51 USD. Citizens of East African Community countries do not require a visa. Yellow fever vaccination certificate required from some countries.
